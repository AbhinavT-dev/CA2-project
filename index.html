The Basics

What Are Microplastics?

Think of them as the dust of our plastic world — invisible flakes smaller than a sesame seed.

Microscopically Tiny

Any plastic piece smaller than 5 millimeters is called a microplastic. The most dangerous ones — called nanoplastics — are smaller than 1 micrometer, making them invisible even under most microscopes.

They're Everywhere Now

Scientists have found microplastics in tap water, bottled water, oceans, arctic snow, clouds, and even in human blood, lungs, and placentas. There is no "safe" water source that is completely free of them today.

3 Types You Need to Know

Fragments — broken shards from larger plastic items.   Fibers — synthetic clothing threads shed during laundry.   Beads — added intentionally to cosmetics and toothpastes.

6 Root Causes

How Do They Get Into Your Water?

There are 6 main pathways that bring microplastics from our everyday plastic use into your drinking glass.

1. Plastic Bottle Caps

Every time you unscrew a plastic bottle cap, friction grinds tiny plastic shards directly into the water. This alone can add hundreds of thousands of particles per liter.

2. Synthetic Clothing Laundry

Washing polyester, nylon, or acrylic clothes releases up to 700,000 synthetic fibers per wash. These fibers pass through wastewater treatment and enter rivers and tap water supplies.

3. Industrial Waste & Pellets

Factories that make plastic products use small raw plastic pellets called "nurdles." Accidental spills of these pellets contaminate rivers that feed municipal water treatment plants.

4. Old Plastic Pipes

Aging PVC and plastic plumbing pipes inside buildings slowly shed particles as water flows through them. The older the building, the higher the risk from pipe degradation.

5. Air & Rainwater Fallout

Plastic particles float in the air and fall with rain into open water reservoirs and wells. Scientists have even detected microplastics in clouds above mountains and in arctic snow.

6. Personal Care Products

Microbeads in exfoliating face washes, toothpaste, and scrubs wash down your drain and pass straight through water treatment plants — they're too small to be filtered out.

Health Impact

What Happens to Your Body?

Once microplastics enter your body, they don't just pass through — some stay, accumulate, and trigger biological responses.

Heart & Blood

Found in blood vessels. Linked to higher risk of heart attack and stroke (2024 study in NEJM).

Lungs & Breathing

Airborne microplastics inhaled into lungs. Linked to inflammation and reduced lung capacity.

Gut & Digestion

Irritates gut lining, disrupts gut bacteria balance, and may worsen inflammatory bowel conditions.

Brain & Hormones

Chemicals in plastics (like BPA and phthalates) act as hormone disruptors, affecting development and mood.

Babies & Unborn Children

Microplastics have been detected in human placentas and umbilical cord blood, meaning babies are exposed before they are even born. Considered especially alarming by WHO.

We Are All Affected

A 2024 study in the New England Journal of Medicine found microplastics inside arterial plaques of people with heart disease — and those with more plastic had 4.5× higher risk of heart attack or stroke. This is no longer a future concern. It is happening now.

How To Protect Yourself

The good news: you can dramatically reduce your exposure with simple, affordable changes today.

Instant Free Actions (Do These Today!)

Switch to Glass or Steel Bottles
Eliminate the #1 source of plastic in your drinking water. One-time cost, used for years.
Boil Your Tap Water
Boiling hard tap water + straining through a coffee filter removes up to 90% of microplastics. Completely free!
Stop Using Plastic Kettles
Boiling water in plastic kettles releases microplastics. Switch to glass or stainless steel immediately.
Use Loose Leaf Tea
Plastic mesh pyramid teabags release up to 11,000 microplastic particles per cup when steeped in hot water.
Never Microwave in Plastic
Heating plastic in a microwave dramatically accelerates the release of micro and nanoplastics into your food.
Use a Laundry Bag Filter
A Guppyfriend bag or Cora Ball in your washing machine captures up to 90% of synthetic clothing fibers before they reach drains.
